07 — process
iyO · UX Pillars & Art Direction

Five pillars to lock the brand.

  • Before touching a pixel, we defined five words that had to hold the brand together.
  • Each pillar had its own keywords and its own visual translation — so designers, devs, and the client were reading the same map.
  • Every decision — typography, motion, imagery, UI — was tested against them.
Pillar 01
Clarity

Make the product feel personal, bold, and new — without losing its professional, premium poise.

PersonalBoldExcitingNovelCuriosity

→ Clear glass UI, glass-morphism, transparent typography, human-centered product photography, minimal hamburger menu.

Pillar 02
Presence

The product is invisible while you wear it. The site had to feel the same — calm, focused, with a rhythm you can breathe to.

BreathableAmbientFocusedRhythmCalm attention

→ Breathing motion in typography, WebGL audio visualizers, zen and nature imagery, neomorphic breathable elements.

Pillar 03
Emotion

The product is a voice in your ear. Everything on screen had to feel like that voice — intimate, trusted, human.

ConversationHuman interfaceLanguage, tone, moodTrusting voiceIntimate

→ Voice-based WebGL interactions, soft gradient cursor on mouse move, generative gradient animations, audio-centric branding.

Pillar 04
Intuition

You shouldn't have to think about the interface. It should feel natural, alive, one step ahead of you.

Feeling interactionsNatural and aliveConversationEffortless navigation

→ Circular shapes and typography, sound-reactive kinetic typefaces, transparent UI over natural-world photography, focus-from-opacity hierarchy.

Pillar 05
Spaciality

It's spatial audio. The interface had to feel dimensional — sound shaping space, space shaping the layout.

Sound and spaceSoundwavesDimensional layeringImmersive motionSpatial storytelling

→ Interactive soundwaves, circular generative patterns, 3D letters zooming through space, immersive exploded-model views, spatial-audio visuals.

How to land it: read Clarity · Presence · Emotion · Intuition · Spaciality out loud as a rhythm. Then say "each pillar drove its own art direction" — and only dive deep on one (Presence or Emotion are the easiest to demo live).

12 — the hard part
iyO · Problems we solved

Three things that broke.

  • The craft layer — where brand design and engineering had to agree.
Problem 1 · 3D mesh too heavy
  • The mic-grid as real geometry was too much for the GPU — frame drops.
  • Fix: swap real geometry for a normal-map texture on a simple mesh.
  • Visually it still "punches through" the surface like real 3D, at a fraction of the cost.
Problem 2 · Hero SVGs too heavy
  • The hero SVGs were beautiful but huge — tens of MB of paths.
  • They killed the first paint.
  • Fix: simplified paths, kept only the load-bearing ones.
  • Added motion to the reduced paths — consistency of the pattern system, at a fraction of the weight.
Problem 3 · Color target across 3 systems
  • Requirement: click a color variant → all product images AND the 3D shader update.
  • Color data lives in Shopify (inventory), UI lives in Webflow, 3D lives in WebGL.
  • Fix: custom bridge — Shopify emits the color as a data-attribute through Webflow's Liquid binding, Webflow passes it as a uniform into the WebGL shader.
  • One click = three systems synced.
Why this matters for Metalab: brand design that survives contact with engineering. This is the slide where I show I can ship, not just design.

19 — receipts
Metalab · Performance

The numbers.

What I measured
  • Ran Lighthouse three times on the current live site.
  • Performance scores: 49 / 52 / 47. Average ~50. Low.
Where the problem comes from
  • One problem, but heavy: Largest Contentful Paint.
  • The site loads 22.3 MB of images on open — PNG and JPG, unoptimized.
  • PNG and JPG are legacy formats: large files, slow decoding, no modern benefits.
  • WebP (standard since 2010, supported by every browser) is 5–10× lighter at the same visual quality.
My fix
  • Converted all images to WebP.
  • Added 5 more projects than the current site shows (Windsurf, Upwork, Athletic, Genies, Pitch).
  • Total weight now: 1.8 MB.
The punchline: more work on screen (+5 projects), ~12× lighter (22.3 MB → 1.8 MB). Let the number land. Don't talk over it.
