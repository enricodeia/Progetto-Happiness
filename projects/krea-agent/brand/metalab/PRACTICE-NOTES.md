## 4. The signature engagement shape

Reading patterns across case studies, Metalab projects fall into 4 archetypes:

### Archetype 1 — "Find the form" (founding-stage)
Founders with a thesis, not yet a product. Metalab gets pulled in to translate vision → MVP. Brand often co-emerges. Examples: Midjourney, Suno, How We Feel, Slack (in 2014), Coinbase (early), Bardeen, Otter, Notarize, Stir, Genies.

### Archetype 2 — "Modernize the giant" (mid/late stage)
Established company with legacy product debt asks Metalab for a top-to-bottom rebuild. Often includes engineering. Examples: TripAdvisor, Yahoo Sports, NerdWallet, Walmart (multi-year), DocuSign, Insider, GoPuff, Crypto.com (scaling phase), Everlywell, Exiger.

### Archetype 3 — "Brand-led product launch"
A brand needs to ship something digital that *embodies* the brand, not just hosts it. Marketing site + product flow integrated. Examples: Calvin Klein, Cometeer, Webflow, Genies, Atomic Habits, Fulcrum, Modular, Together.ai.

### Archetype 4 — "Trusted partner / R&D"
Long-running relationship with a big-tech client where Metalab plays a fractional design / strategy role. Examples: Apple ("various projects since founding"), Google, Meta, Walmart, Nike, Uber.

**The Senior Brand Designer (Marketing) role you're applying for almost certainly sits inside Archetype 3 work** — and the design test brief is most likely a fictional Archetype 3 prompt: a brand that needs a marketing site or campaign that *makes the brand legible to the world*.

## 5. Patterns visible across case studies (visual & methodological)

### 5.1 Brand-product integration
- Brand identity and product UI are designed to share *the same DNA*, not as separate deliverables.
- A Metalab brand is almost never a brand book sitting alone — it always has a digital surface (web, app, or both) where it actually proves it works.

### 5.2 Editorial typography as the dominant brand voice
- Across Modular, Suno, Atomic Habits, Webflow, Calvin Klein, Together.ai: high-contrast display typography sets the tone, not custom illustration.
- Metalab clients tend to use **one expressive display face + one rational sans**, mirroring Metalab's own PP Eiko + Basis Grotesque setup.

### 5.3 Photography over illustration
- Real subjects, real environments, restrained color grading.
- Illustration is rare and is reserved for moments of explanation (onboarding, edge cases), not as a brand pillar.

### 5.4 Motion as a craftsmanship signal
- Many case studies foreground motion (Suno, Fanatics Live, Headspace, TED, Nike, Crypto.com).
- Motion is often the deliverable that shows Metalab "level" most clearly — interview implication: motion-aware static designs > pixel-perfect static designs.

### 5.5 Engineering as a partner, not a downstream
- Repeated mention of "design + engineering" engagements signals a non-tossed-over-the-wall handoff culture.
- Implication for you: your **design + React** ability is *exactly* the differentiator that fits this culture.

### 5.6 Strategy first
- Phrases like "vision," "strategy," "product strategy," "concepting," "ideation" appear in roughly half of case studies.
- The studio sells thinking, not just craft. The Senior Brand Designer is expected to have a point of view, not just hands.

## 7. Implications for the Senior Brand Designer (Marketing) interview

### What they will look for in the design test
1. **Strategy before craft.** The first 1–2 pages of your deliverable should be your reading of the brief, the audience, and the brand thesis. If you skip to visuals, you've lost.
2. **A brand that ships somewhere.** Don't deliver a brand book. Deliver a brand + a marketing site (or campaign) that demonstrates the brand in motion.
3. **Editorial typography fluency.** The work should look comfortable next to Modular, Suno, Webflow, or Calvin Klein. Not necessarily *like* them — but in the same conversation.
4. **Motion-aware thinking.** Even on static deliverables, indicate motion intent (annotations, mini storyboards, or one Lottie/MP4).
5. **Engineering plausibility.** Your designs should look like things you could ship in code — not Behance fantasy. Bonus if you actually ship a small piece of it.

### What you can leverage that other candidates can't
- **You design AND build in React.** Most senior brand designers can't ship a working prototype. You can. Use this as your differentiator in the design test cover note: *"I included a small live prototype because in this culture I assumed designs that ship beat designs that don't."*
- **You already have a Vite + React boilerplate** with the Metalab brand kit pre-loaded. When the brief lands, you can ship a real, deployed Vercel URL in hours, not days.

### What you should NOT do
- Mention this `BRAND-REFERENCE.md` or `PRACTICE-NOTES.md` to Metalab. These are your private prep documents. Showing them would feel performative, not useful.
- Mimic `metalab.com` colors (`#584dff`) or fonts in the design test. The test is for a hypothetical client, not for Metalab. Use a *different* signature color, a *different* type pairing — show you can build a brand from scratch, not copy theirs.
- Over-explain in the deliverable. Three paragraphs of strategy beats ten. Their case studies are short.
